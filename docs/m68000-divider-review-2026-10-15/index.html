MC68000 divider ordering review, 15 October 2026

Conclusion¶

Follow-up core correction: m68k commit 275b2e6 preserves the MC68000 destination and sets overflow for signed $80000000 / -1. It also exposes the measured early DIVS poll through AddressBus::ipl_poll_internal, taking and retaining a fresh sample two CPU clocks before the unchanged final prefetch. Copperline implements the hook; it does not reuse the preceding operand access’s sample. Other CPU models retain their existing behavior. All four m68k contribution checks pass, including the new clocked-bus IRQ and overflow regressions. The review and counts below describe the preceding core version unless explicitly marked as a post-fix replay.

The remaining saved-PC differences must not all be attributed to the host. The probe waits in TST.W DONE(A5) and a conditional branch after the marker. A source comparison also finds an ordinary-instruction poll discrepancy: WinUAE’s cycle-exact memory TST handler samples IPL before the operand read, whereas m68k’s exec_tst currently leaves the boundary sample to the final access. Isolate that waiting-loop poll point separately from the DIV/marker boundary and from host IRQ propagation. It is not changed by the DIVS fix.

Post-fix Copperline replay¶

The rebuilt release executable replayed the unchanged accepted v3 ROM against all 2,560 physical A500 records, with zero excluded launches, timeouts or skips. All division results now agree (256 mismatches before, zero after). Saved-PC and handler-beam mismatches are 452 (446 before), including 40 DIV/marker boundary mismatches (34 before). Final marker values still all agree. The early DIVS poll removes an accidental cancellation in six boundary cases; it does not close the common recognition discrepancy affecting ordinary DIVU/DIVS. These results must not be described as fully accurate end-to-end divider interrupts. The core’s internal poll placement is separately checked by clocked-bus tests, including IRQ arrivals between the poll and prefetch.

Physical verification following the source review¶

Two new One ROM runs on the PAL A500 produced byte-identical 2,560-trial packets. WinUAE Best Compatibility matches every checked observable in all 2,560 matched-launch records: saved interrupt PC, marker at IRQ entry, handler beam sample, final D0 and final marker. There were no timeouts, skips or excluded launch pairs. The matrix covers all four DIVS sign pairs, zero dividend, signed quotient overflow without early absolute overflow, early absolute overflow and two DIVU controls, with display DMA disabled.

The sign-pair boundary measurements support WinUAE’s special early DIVS poll. They also establish that the current Copperline/m68k combination is not fully equivalent: 446 saved-PC/handler-beam differences include 34 cases crossing the DIV/following-marker boundary. The remaining saved-PC differences occur in the subsequent waiting loop. All 256 signed $80000000 / -1 results differ: hardware and WinUAE preserve $80000000, while m68k writes zero. That behavior predates the ordering correction. These are overlapping counts, not additive independent failures.

Ordinary DIVU and DIVS boundaries also differ, so implementing only the special DIVS sample cannot resolve all observations. Separate the host IRQ propagation/sample mechanism from CPU-specific poll points before changing general latency. No CPU code was changed during this verification.

Source comparison¶

WinUAE cpuemu_13.cpp, op_80c0_13_ff and the other DIVU addressing-mode handlers, advance the divider’s internal clocks before the final instruction prefetch. The DIVS handlers, including op_81c0_13_ff, do likewise. The MC68000 cycle calculations in newcpu_common.cpp exclude the final four-clock prefetch; m68k’s calculations include it, and finish_m68000_div subtracts those four clocks before synchronizing. These conventions agree.

There is a significant DIVS exception to the ordinary interrupt poll point. WinUAE getDivs68kCycles reports an extra case when the dividend is nonnegative and the divisor is negative, after excluding the early absolute overflow path. Its generated handler advances all but the last two internal CPU clocks, calls ipl_fetch_next, advances the remaining two clocks, and then performs the final prefetch. Thus this poll is two CPU clocks (one PAL A500 colour clock) earlier than the ordinary prefetch-associated poll.

m68k’s divider helper always advances the entire internal interval before prefetch. Its AddressBus contract normally takes the boundary IPL sample from the last bus access. There is no operand-dependent early DIVS sample in this helper. Consequently the patch matches the final bus-access placement but does not implement WinUAE’s special DIVS IPL timing. An interrupt changing near that boundary could be taken after a different instruction even though uncontended division duration and final prefetch timing agree. This is a source-level difference; the subsequent hardware sweep above supports WinUAE’s boundary behavior.

There are also pre-existing differences outside this ordering correction:

  • For signed $80000000 / -1, m68k explicitly writes zero to the destination and clears overflow. WinUAE’s MC68000 path preserves the destination and sets overflow. The m68k behavior is present in this commit’s parent.

  • m68k updates the division result and flags after its final prefetch; WinUAE updates them before it. A final-prefetch bus fault therefore needs a dedicated exception-state comparison. This relative writeback ordering was already present before the correction and was not validated by the A500 elapsed-time experiments.

These differences should not be presented as regressions introduced by moving the prefetch. They do prevent a blanket claim that fixed m68k DIVU and DIVS match WinUAE in every respect.

Earlier hardware evidence and its limits¶

The saved aligned CPU sweep contains 1,792 scheduled trials. Against the physical A500, fixed Copperline has zero elapsed-time mismatches among 1,732 safe same-launch pairs; WinUAE has zero among 1,640; the earlier Copperline capture has 655 among 1,685. Pair counts differ because unsafe beam samples and unequal launches are excluded independently for each capture.

The six-bitplane, 64-division loops agree across all three systems: normal DIVU 4763–4767 colour clocks, worst-case DIVU 5102, and DIVS 5249–5257, including loop and readback overhead. These figures are saved measurements, not newly rerun results. See a500-timing-measurements-2026-10-14.md and the probe repository’s docs/rom-timing-isolation-2026-10-14.md.

The original probe’s aligned DIVS kernel uses dividend $12345678 and divisor $789a. Its original DIVS/IRQ kernel also uses a positive divisor. Neither covers the negative-divisor early IPL sample. The new m68k bus-sequence tests cover register, immediate and memory operands and overflow paths, but do not inject interrupts or test prefetch faults. No external MC68000 bus capture has yet established the divider’s physical prefetch boundary.

Before claiming fully accurate divider IPL timing¶

The initial measurement plan below is retained for context. Steps 1-3 were subsequently executed without display DMA, as recorded above; the probe report contains the updated remaining plan. Display-DMA contention and external prefetch capture remain untested by the new sweep.

  1. Extend the One ROM IRQ probe with all four operand sign combinations, keeping the quotient in range. Include zero dividend with a negative divisor, signed quotient overflow without early absolute overflow, and early absolute overflow as a separate control.

  2. Sweep the IRQ assertion across the last internal clocks and final prefetch. Record the saved interrupt PC to distinguish interruption immediately after DIV from interruption after its following marker. Use identical actual launch phases and inspect both CPU clock parities.

  3. Repeat with display DMA disabled first, then with bitplane contention. Compare physical A500, WinUAE Best Compatibility, and Copperline. Use the scope to confirm final-prefetch placement; do not infer the internal IPL latch directly from an external bus waveform.

  4. If hardware confirms the early sample, design an explicit timed IPL sampling facility in the CPU/bus interface. Do not move the prefetch two clocks earlier to imitate the interrupt behavior: WinUAE keeps the prefetch at its ordinary position. Add a bus/IRQ regression that tests both poll timing and unchanged prefetch timing.

  5. Validate the signed minimum-dividend overflow result and final-prefetch fault state separately. Keep these independent from the bus-ordering PR unless its scope is deliberately expanded.

For the existing PR, describe the validated change as moving the MC68000 divider’s internal interval before its final prefetch, with improved ordinary IPL placement. Explicitly disclose the unimplemented special DIVS poll point. The present evidence supports that focused correction, not a claim of complete divider or interrupt equivalence.
