CIA physical output and timer readback stages

The A500 kind66 and address-coded scope probes distinguish PB output, timer readback, ICR flags, physical CIA /IRQ, Paula INTREQR and physical CPU IPL. CPU interrupts are masked in the scope kernels. These are distinct observables; a PB discrepancy does not justify shifting the timer or CPU recognition.

Physical PB stage¶

Four-channel-qualified CIA-B captures show PHI2 PB6/PB7 output one E-clock (10 CPU clocks) before the timer flag and ordinary /IRQ assertion. Copperline commits this physical output stage; PRB transfer reads observe the same pins. This replaces the previous transfer-only PRB preview. Pulse expiry and adjacent pulse merging remain explicit, and the event scheduler visits the PB boundary. CNT/cascade timer-B output retains its existing stage: these captures directly qualify the PHI2 path, not all timer input modes or CIA revisions.

Automatic reload and old-counter carry¶

A one-shot high-byte write preserves a carry from the old zero counter independently of the newly loaded timer. Timer B now retains this history, matching the existing Timer-A distinction. An old carry does not stop the newly loaded one-shot. Old3/new0 must not create the earlier old-zero carry.

A separate old0/3, new15 test proves event count: after a destructive ICR read, old0 produces two separated physical IRQ assertions near 20 and 180 CPU clocks; old3 produces only the later assertion. This rules out explaining the extended zero-latch PB pulse solely as an output-width artifact. New serialized history fields default to false when absent in older states.

Validation and remaining uncertainty¶

References in amiga-hardware-probes: references/cia-b-four-channel-scope-20261024-v1 (28 captures), references/cia-tb-event-count-20261024-v1 (4 captures), and docs/cia-pin-boundaries-2026-10-24.md (qualification and baseline deltas). The prior kind66 reference remains references/cia-source-v3/run{1,2}.

The combined change repairs 82 qualified scope read differences without a new qualified difference. The earlier 288-case pulse reference repaired 36 cases, but retained a historical difference and unstable rows. Subsequent probes verified stopped-counter history and starting phase: the controlled 576-case matrix matches, and five independently phase-selected refresh observations reproduce the model’s extra E tick and resulting pulse inversion. The old unqualified comparison is not evidence for another PB retiming.

The later ICR/underflow sweep matches all 2,048 selected-source flag and control cases. Eight four-channel CIA-B captures provide 52 valid one-shot boundary observations: ordinary IRQ uses falling E, while a raced request uses rising E. The measured race-minus-control interval is approximately 5.90 CPU clocks, consistent with the modeled six-clock difference. These controls do not establish every external-input or continuous/cascade boundary.

Physical /IRQ precedes IPL6 by about eight CPU clocks. The implemented output/input/CPU stages are described in the timing model. Request and aperture comparisons match 640/640 and 1,280/1,280 cases; conditioned keyboard startup controls retain their qualified matches. The unchanged DEFAULT_IRQ_LATENCY_CCK=6 remains separate from source propagation.

The software ICR sweep’s elapsed-E witness is a separate unresolved observation: 1,898 matches, 36 stable differences and 114 unstable cases. It includes a custom beam read before arm; exact starting-phase coverage is incomplete. Flag agreement and physical boundary checks do not waive those timing results. Historical broad-suite exclusions also remain exclusions, not hardware matches.

Current evidence in the sibling probe repository: docs/cia-pb-phase-2026-10-25.md, docs/cia-icr-race-2026-10-25.md, references/cia-pb-phase-chip-20261025-v1/, references/cia-pb-gated-scope-20261025-v1/, references/cia-pb-tb1-scope-20261025-v1/, and references/cia-icr-race-scope-20261025-v2/. Captures retain exact source/ROM fingerprints, units and qualification rules; no trace fitting is used.

Non-timer request regression controls¶

Two independent 64-case A500 captures with keyboard and DF0 attached match serial-output and clocked-TOD controls on both CIAs. They distinguish a masked pending source, enable after acknowledgement, acknowledgement of Paula while the CIA source remains asserted, and acknowledgement of the CIA while Paula still retains its latch. The selected-source flag must be present; unrelated raw ICR flags are retained separately. No timing shifts or exclusions are used. The native bus regression repeats these orders and checks a fresh reassertion.

Two qualified runs each match all 72 cases, including eight real CIA-B disk-index FLAG controls. These cover masked pending flags, enable after acknowledgement, held-source reassertion after Paula acknowledgement, and final source/request clearing. Positive source witnesses are mandatory; no exclusions or timing shifts are used.

The native FLAG test injects a synthetic input; the hardware reference verifies the settled real-index path. Neither establishes electrical FLAG-to-IRQ latency or the CIA-A parallel-ACK path. Complete packets, source fingerprints and verified restoration are in references/cia-external-pr-complete-20261026-v1/ in the sibling probe repository; see docs/cia-floppy-flag-2026-10-26.md there for the before/after fixture evidence. No emulation change was required by the measurements.
