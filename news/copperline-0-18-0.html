Copperline 0.18.0: The CD32 FMV module, calibrated drive timing, a modem on Paula, and import-uae

Copperline 0.18.0 is the CD release. The CD32's Full Motion Video module - Commodore's MPEG cartridge - is emulated end to end, so Video CDs play and FMV-aware games run their MPEG sequences on an emulated CD32 exactly as they do with the real hardware cartridge plugged into the rear expansion bay. Underneath it, the CD32 drive model has been calibrated against a real PAL CD32 with a purpose-built probe disc and a filmed cold boot: spin-up, seek times, and command pacing are now measured quantities, and the emulated cold boot tracks the real machine's to within 50 milliseconds - close enough that Fightin' Spirit, which relies on the exact boot-time memory layout, boots from its unmodified disc for the very first time.

Around the optical drive story: CDXL video plays from cooked ISOs, Nero NRG images mount directly, cue sheets accept WAVE and MP3 audio tracks, a new copperline-import-uae tool converts WinUAE, Amiberry, and FS-UAE configurations with zero guesswork, a Hayes-compatible modem answers on the serial port, run-ahead trims input latency, and AGA sprite, fetch, and palette fidelity has been pushed to pixel-exact against the vAmigaTS references. As always, it is a hardware-behaviour release: compatibility fixes model the underlying Amiga subsystems rather than applying per-title hacks.

The CD32 Full Motion Video module

Setting [machine] fmv_rom fits Commodore's MPEG cartridge as the first Zorro II board in a CD32. We model the C-Cube CL450 video processor and LSI Logic L64111 audio decoder interfaces, the module's onboard RAM and ROM, and the analogue video keying path that mixes decoded MPEG video with Denise's native graphics output, scaling Video CD frames across the full capture aperture.

MPEG-1 video is decoded through a safe pure-Rust port of plmpeg (CopperlineHQ/plmpeg) and MPEG Layer II audio through Symphonia. Video CDs play smoothly, and FMV titles run their full video sequences - Cannon Fodder's famous filmed intro plays before the game loads and runs to gameplay completely deterministically. MODE2/2336 and MODE2/2352 tracks are handled seamlessly across CUE, NRG, and CHD images alike.

The 256 KiB v40.30 cartridge ROM is yours to supply, just like a Kickstart ROM. One note for now: booting a CD with the module fitted requires a genuine Commodore Kickstart ROM, because the cartridge carries its own Kickstart-era cd.device that takes over the boot path from the bundled AROS ROM; an upstream AROS update to handle this is in progress.

A CD32 drive calibrated against real hardware

Until now, CD32 drive timing in emulators has mostly been based on rough estimates. For this release, we built a dedicated probe disc (tools/cd32-probe, included in the repository along with the measurement tables in its README) and filmed cold boots on a real PAL CD32 to turn guesswork into hard measurements.

The new drive model includes:

  • A measured spin-up gate on the first lead-in dump (8.8 seconds cold, 3.5 seconds warm).
  • TOC dumps that complete before subsequent queued commands can act.
  • A measured seek curve that replaces the old single-slot seek approximation.
  • TX DMA that folds to its 256-byte page the way Kickstart's producer expects.
  • Unmapped address space billed at its measured bus cost.

The emulated cold boot now enters the startup-sequence at 14.36 emulated seconds against 14.31 filmed seconds on real hardware. And because the boot animation never runs while a bootable disc is spinning up, chip RAM is laid out exactly as on the real machine: Fightin' Spirit boots unpatched to the credits, and discs inserted at the idle screen or mid-animation now boot cleanly, just like closing the physical CD lid.

CDXL from cooked ISOs and expanded CD formats

Reading from a bare .iso image now synthesizes complete raw Mode 1 sectors - including EDC and P/Q parity bytes - on the fly. This means CDXL video streams cleanly from cooked ISO files, and CDTV-trademark boots work properly on the CD32 profile, with cached TOC transport paced independently of optical sector delivery.

Image support is also broader: Nero .nrg images can now be mounted anywhere a CUE sheet is accepted, and CUE sheets can reference compressed WAVE and MP3 audio tracks with full support for PREGAP and POSTGAP directives. Ripped games with compressed soundtracks play without needing manual decompression or re-mastering.

Import UAE configurations: copperline-import-uae

Switching emulators shouldn't mean re-creating dozens of configurations by hand. Simon Dick (@sidick) contributed copperline-import-uae, a standalone conversion tool that translates WinUAE, Amiberry, and FS-UAE configurations into native Copperline TOML.

It maps machine models, CPU and FPU choices, RAM layouts, chipsets, Kickstart ROMs, floppy drives (converting FS-UAE swap lists into playlists), hard drives, directory mounts, CD images, RTC, audio, joystick port mappings, and expansion boards like [lide], [scsi], and [toccata].

Best of all, it is completely transparent: approximations are commented directly above the generated lines, and any unsupported options are enumerated at the bottom of the output file so you can easily see what was intentionally skipped versus what might need manual attention. The generated configuration is validated against Copperline's own configuration loader before writing. See docs/import-uae for usage instructions.

A Hayes modem on Paula's serial port

[serial] mode = "modem" attaches a full Hayes AT-command modem to Paula's serial port. Terminal software like Term and NComm, as well as BBS doors expecting real AT dial commands, result codes, carrier detection, and the classic +++ escape sequence, now get authentic modem responses rather than a raw byte pipe.

The modem handles inbound connections (RING, S0 auto-answer, ATA), dials outbound destinations from a config-file phonebook, negotiates Telnet NVT for connecting to modern BBS servers over TCP, and persists AT&W profiles to a sidecar file. It also includes a WiModem232 compatibility layer verified against the original hardware manual, allowing published community setup guides to work without changes. For automated testing, a scripted-session transport (--serial-session) replays dial-out sessions byte-for-byte in headless runs. Contributed by Simon Dick (@sidick); see the modem documentation for details.

Run-ahead input latency reduction

For demanding action games where every millisecond counts, [emulation] run_ahead_frames (0 to 4 frames, selectable via --run-ahead or under Emulation Settings > Run Ahead) trims perceived input latency below that of real CRT hardware. Each display refresh commits one normal frame, takes an in-memory snapshot, silently executes the requested future frames with the latest controller inputs, presents the final result, and rolls back to the snapshot.

Safety is built-in: whenever an emulated frame could touch host state that cannot be rewound (such as writable floppy or hard disk images, an active serial connection, session recording, or connected CCP clients), run-ahead is automatically paused and the reason is displayed in the menu. If a speculative burst cannot restore its anchor, the feature safely disables itself rather than corrupting the timeline.

AGA and display fidelity

Lisa's sprite serializer is now modeled in 35 ns clock samples. SuperHires (SHRES) sprites (SPRES=11) are now distinctly separated from hires sprites, reproducing the exact 4:4:2:1 width staircase photographed on a real Amiga 1200. Both SHRES playfield halves also survive priority sorting and pixel composition.

Alice's wide-fetch address phases are fully modeled - including FMODE 10 page-mode duplication and unaligned aliasing - along with per-resolution DMA bandwidth ceilings, Lisa's banked BPLCON2.RDRAM palette readback, and the one-hires-pixel COLOR write delay. In the vAmigaTS test sweep, the two worst AGA test cases dropped from 16.179% and 70.931% mismatch down to a clean 0.000%, and all AGA golden probe tests in the repository are now cross-validated against vAmiga 5.0b1.

The browser build grows a second writable drive

The WebAssembly build at copperline.dev/try now wires up DF1 alongside DF0 and keeps floppy images writable in browser memory. Multi-disk installations and disk-to-disk saves work completely client-side in your browser session, with modified disk images downloadable at any time.

Hardware accuracy fixes

  • Vertical display window set/reset flop: DIWSTRT.V sets the window, DIWSTOP.V clears it, and the last raster line resets it against standard line limits. Empty windows (DIWSTRT.V == DIWSTOP.V) never open, and flop state survives save states and frame wraps. Kang Fu's interlaced CD32 menus and Nexus 7's scene transitions now render cleanly.
  • AGA sprite palette banks: Corrected a transposition where even sprites read colors through BPLCON4's ESPRM nibble and odd/attached pairs through OSPRM. Copper fetches can now ride the line-end refresh slot, and the blitter deadline predictor mirrors the refresh carve-out (verified against Nexus 7).
  • DDFSTRT rewrite timing: Repositioning DDFSTRT behind the beam mid-line prevents comparator matching so that the line fetch drops properly, fixing plane desyncs in Microcosm's control panel.
  • Dual-playfield sprite priority: Sprites are judged against the winning playfield's BPLCON2 priority code rather than a fixed field, fixing the bin Junior hides behind in Chuck Rock II.
  • COPJMP strobe reads: Strobe reads now fire correctly and return floating-bus residue, smoothing animations in Sleepwalker. First-line BPLCON1 scrolling fills bitplane DMA blocks from pre-window fetches, eliminating the corner notches in Super Skidmarks on CD32.
  • HAM8 resolver: Preserved the history-dependent resolver, fixing a 0.17.0 regression where an indexed-cache shortcut broke HAM8 scenes.
  • Paula disk sync: WORDSYNC reads re-frame on every DSKSYNC match, and disk DMA armed without an inserted disk remains pending until real flux transitions arrive rather than completing on phantom data.
  • Akiko & AROS: CDINTREQ reads return raw latches, RX DMA wraps within its 256-byte page, and data-track audio plays return real firmware errors. The bundled AROS ROM was updated with upstream fixes for cd.device interrupts, ciab.resource handshakes, and stale IORequest handling.
  • Picasso II BitBLT: Corrected transposed NOR and NAND raster op encodings, BLT address counter stopping positions, and source-end derivation (contributed by Bernie Innocenti).

Desktop, tooling and platform

  • Fuzzing & parser hardening: New fuzz targets for untrusted media headers (CHD) and save-state streams prevent unbounded allocations and safely validate compressed maps.
  • Supply-chain CI: Added cargo deny checks for dependency licenses and advisories, automated MSRV builds, and scheduled workspace dependency updates.
  • cputest conformance: Nightly WinUAE CPU test suites now propagate their verdict directly to CI exit codes.
  • m68k 0.11.6: Core update with driveless machine smoke testing in CI.
  • Launcher improvements: Reorganized Storage, Video/Display, and Serial tabs (boot priorities grouped with drives, proper host/port inputs for serial).
  • Gamepad & UI: Added a Quit entry to the main menu, hold-Select to quit on standard pads, and alternate directional bindings during calibration.

Compatibility notes

Save states from 0.17.0 do not load in 0.18.0. The FMV module, calibrated drive timing, and display window flip-flop all changed the serialized machine state, incrementing the state version from 61 to 70. Please re-create snapshots after upgrading.

CD32 and CDTV default to zero floppy drives ([floppy] drives = 0), matching original hardware. If you need a floppy drive on these models, add drives = 1 in your config or pass --floppy-drives 1.

lide.device IDE boards now autoboot out of the box. Fitted boards default to the bundled open-source ROMs by Matt Harlum (LIV2). Each drive slot now uses its own drive0..drive3 key with individual boot priorities.

Configuration keys are strictly additive: No existing keys were removed or renamed. New options include [machine] fmv_rom, [emulation] run_ahead_frames, [serial] mode = "modem", and .nrg / WAVE / MP3 cue audio tracks.
